Data & methodology

How this atlas is built

Every record lists its sources. Missing values are explicit null and are never invented; estimated values are flagged with *_estimated: true.

Confidence tiers

Confirmed

Operational and officially documented: operator page, regulatory filing, or OpenStreetMap / Wikidata with coordinates.

Probable

Strong evidence the site exists or is under construction, but not officially confirmed at that exact location (news, satellite imagery, permits).

Theorized

Announced, rumoured, planned or speculative — for example proposed AI mega-campuses.

Sources

  • OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API: telecom=data_center and building=data_center features.
  • Wikidata SPARQL: items that are an instance of data center (Q671224) with coordinates.
  • Curated research: hyperscaler region pages (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle, IBM, Alibaba), colocation providers (Equinix, Digital Realty, NTT, Switch, QTS, Vantage, CyrusOne, Iron Mountain, STACK, Colt, Global Switch, DATA4, Aruba…), Meta / Apple / xAI / OpenAI campuses, government and HPC sites, and crypto-mining facilities.

How records are merged

Raw features are normalised to a common schema, de-duplicated when two records fall within 500 m and share a name or operator, validated, and exported as JSON, GeoJSON and pre-computed statistics. The Python pipeline in scripts/ reproduces the dataset end to end.

Limitations

  • Cloud region markers are placed at the region's primary metro area; a region spans several availability zones whose exact locations are not public.
  • Power capacity is only shown when publicly reported; most sites have null. Headline MW totals are therefore a lower bound.
  • The estimated annual energy figure assumes the known capacity runs at 100% for 8,760 hours — an upper bound for those sites, not a measurement.
  • OpenStreetMap coverage is community-driven and uneven across countries.

Contributing

Found an error or a missing site? Records can be added or corrected through pull requests — see CONTRIBUTING.md. The dataset is open and the source is on GitHub.
